Road Connections

Four major highways open the Northeast in every direction.

Route 1, Interstate 287, the Garden State Parkway and the New Jersey Turnpike give North Edison an unusually broad road network, serving local Central New Jersey travel as well as long distance routes toward New York, the Jersey Shore, Pennsylvania, New England, upstate New York and the Mid Atlantic.

Four Roads, Four Different Roles

The strength is not one highway. It is the network.

North Edison's road access works because several major corridors intersect within the same Central New Jersey transportation system. Route 1 handles the local and regional spine. I-287 opens the west and northwest. The Garden State Parkway runs through New Jersey and into the New York Thruway system. The New Jersey Turnpike places the area on the larger I-95 East Coast corridor.

The Core Network

Route 1. I-287. Garden State Parkway. New Jersey Turnpike.

Each highway solves a different travel problem. Together they create a road network that reaches far beyond Edison.

US 1

Route 1: Central New Jersey's local regional spine.

U.S. Route 1 runs directly through Edison and links major commercial, employment and residential areas across Middlesex County. NJDOT describes the Edison to Woodbridge section as part of a critical transportation facility and has reconstructed the corridor between I-287 and the Garden State Parkway to improve capacity, traffic flow and safety.

For North Edison, Route 1 is the practical distributor. It connects with I-287 in Edison and continues south toward New Brunswick, the Princeton area and Trenton while also linking northward toward Woodbridge and the Parkway corridor.

Primary role: local and regional Central New Jersey movement.
I-287

Interstate 287: the western and northwestern gateway.

I-287 begins in the Edison area at the New Jersey Turnpike, Route 440 and I-287 interchange and runs north through Middlesex, Somerset and Morris counties. NJDOT identifies major connections along the corridor with Route 1, I-78 and I-80 before I-287 reaches the New York State line.

This makes I-287 particularly important when the destination does not lie on the New York to Philadelphia axis. It opens routes toward Bridgewater, Morristown, I-78, I-80, eastern Pennsylvania and the western side of the New York metropolitan region.

Primary role: west, northwest and circumferential regional travel.
GSP

Garden State Parkway: New Jersey from north to south.

The New Jersey Turnpike Authority identifies the Garden State Parkway as a 172 mile limited access toll road extending from the Cape May area to the New York state line at Montvale. It is one of the state's defining north south highways.

Southbound, the Parkway is the principal road corridor toward much of the Jersey Shore. Northbound, it runs toward Union, Essex, Passaic and Bergen counties and connects with the New York State Thruway system. Metropark itself sits directly off the Parkway at Exit 131A, creating an unusually useful road to rail interface.

Primary role: statewide north south travel, Jersey Shore and New York State connection.
NJTP

New Jersey Turnpike: the East Coast interstate spine.

The New Jersey Turnpike forms part of the I-95 corridor and is the major long distance road axis connecting the New York and Philadelphia metropolitan regions through New Jersey. Official Turnpike records identify Interchange 10 in Edison, near milepost 88.

Northbound, the Turnpike leads toward Newark, Newark Liberty International Airport and the greater New York region. Southbound, it leads toward the Philadelphia area and onward to Delaware, Maryland, Washington and the wider I-95 corridor.

Primary role: long distance Northeast and Mid Atlantic travel.
Two Statewide Toll Roads

The Parkway and Turnpike complement each other.

They are both operated by the New Jersey Turnpike Authority, but they serve different travel patterns.

One road stays focused on New Jersey. The other plugs into I-95.

The Garden State Parkway is especially powerful for movement within New Jersey, including the shore counties and the long north south path toward Bergen County and New York State.

The New Jersey Turnpike is the larger interstate route through the state. It is the natural road corridor for travel between the New York metropolitan region, Philadelphia and points farther south along the East Coast.

For North Edison, the advantage is having access to both systems rather than depending on one highway for every type of trip.

North

Hudson Valley, Catskills, Albany, Adirondacks and Montreal corridor.

The Parkway's northern connection to New York's Thruway system turns a New Jersey highway into a gateway to a much larger regional network.

Garden State Parkway into the New York Thruway.

The New York State Thruway Authority operates a dedicated Garden State Parkway Connector linking New Jersey's Parkway with the mainline Thruway, I-87, near Spring Valley. From there, I-87 runs north through the Hudson Valley toward Albany.

Official Thruway interchange listings identify New Paltz, Kingston, Saugerties and Catskill along this northbound corridor, making the Hudson Valley and Catskills part of the same highway network reached from the Parkway.

GSP→NY Thruway I-87→Hudson Valley→Albany

Lake George, Adirondacks and the Canadian border.

At Albany, I-87 continues north as the Adirondack Northway toward Lake George, the Adirondacks, Plattsburgh and the Canadian border. North of the border, the road system continues toward Montreal.

This does not mean every trip should follow an identical route. Traffic and destination can change the best path. The point is the continuity of the northbound highway system from New Jersey into eastern New York and toward Quebec.

I-87→Lake George→Adirondacks→Canada→Montreal
West & Northwest

I-287 opens routes that bypass the New York to Philadelphia axis.

Its value is less about one famous destination and more about how many east west interstates it connects.

I-78 and eastern Pennsylvania.

Northbound I-287 intersects I-78 in Somerset County. That connection opens the Lehigh Valley and additional Pennsylvania routes to the west, while also serving major employment and commercial centers in Somerset and Morris counties.

I-287→I-78→Western NJ→Pennsylvania

I-80 and the Poconos.

Farther north, I-287 intersects I-80. This creates a direct highway path toward the Delaware Water Gap and the Pocono region, while I-80 also continues west across Pennsylvania.

I-287→I-80→Delaware Water Gap→Poconos
Across New York State

Finger Lakes, Syracuse, Rochester, Buffalo and Niagara Falls.

The New York Thruway connects the northbound I-87 corridor at Albany with the I-90 mainline across upstate New York.

I-90 across the center of New York.

The Thruway Authority describes its 426 mile mainline as extending from the New York City region to Buffalo using I-87 and I-90. West of Albany, I-90 serves the Mohawk Valley, Syracuse and Rochester regions and provides access to the broader Finger Lakes area.

The exact exit and secondary road depend on the lake or town being visited, but the Thruway is the principal east west superhighway across this part of the state.

I-87→Albany→I-90→Finger Lakes→Rochester

Buffalo, Niagara Falls and Canada.

At the western end of the state, the Thruway reaches Buffalo. The Authority's Niagara Thruway, I-190, connects from I-90 through Buffalo toward Niagara Falls and international crossings into Canada.

Again, this is a network description rather than a promise of the fastest route from North Edison on every day. Weather, construction and traffic can materially change the best path.

I-90→Buffalo→I-190→Niagara Falls→Canada
South

Jersey Shore on one axis. Philadelphia and Washington on another.

The Parkway and Turnpike divide the southbound travel market neatly.

Garden State Parkway toward the shore.

Southbound Parkway travel leads through Monmouth and Ocean counties and continues down the length of the state toward Atlantic and Cape May counties. For many Jersey Shore destinations, it is the defining highway corridor.

GSP South→Monmouth→Ocean→South Jersey→Cape May

New Jersey Turnpike toward Philadelphia and the Mid Atlantic.

Southbound Turnpike travel links into the Philadelphia region and the larger I-95 network. From there, the interstate corridor continues through Delaware toward Baltimore, Washington and destinations farther south.

NJ Turnpike→Philadelphia→Delaware→Baltimore→Washington
East & Metropolitan Connections

The network also reaches Staten Island and the New York crossings.

Several additional roads extend from the four principal corridors into the larger metropolitan system.

Route 440 and the Outerbridge Crossing.

At the southern end of I-287 in Edison, New Jersey Route 440 continues east toward Perth Amboy and the Outerbridge Crossing. That creates a road connection toward Staten Island and, through New York's network, other boroughs and crossings.

Turnpike connections toward the New York region.

Northbound Turnpike travel provides access toward Newark, Jersey City connections, Lincoln and Holland Tunnel approaches through connecting roadways, and the northern Turnpike alignment toward the George Washington Bridge corridor.

Road Meets Rail

Metropark is where the two systems visibly intersect.

The area's road and rail advantages are not separate stories.

Drive to the corridor, then leave the car behind.

NJ Transit locates Metropark directly off Garden State Parkway Exit 131A. Drivers can approach through the regional road network, park at the station and continue by NJ Transit or Amtrak.

That combination matters for trips where driving all the way into New York, Philadelphia, Washington or another corridor city is less attractive than transferring to rail. It also gives travelers alternatives when congestion, parking or weather makes one mode less practical.

NJ TransitNortheast Corridor commuter rail from the same station.

Road Planning Note

Traffic changes the practical map.

Highway proximity does not guarantee a fixed travel time. New Jersey traffic can vary significantly by hour, season, weather, construction, crashes and special events. NJDOT's 511NJ service provides real time conditions, incidents, construction information, cameras and travel times for state highways, the Garden State Parkway and the New Jersey Turnpike. Check current conditions before longer trips.

Ground Connections: Road

North Edison is connected in more than two directions.

Route 1 handles Central New Jersey. I-287 opens the west and northwest. The Parkway runs toward the Jersey Shore and into New York State. The Turnpike joins the I-95 corridor toward New York, Philadelphia and Washington. Their combined value is a road network that can be entered close to North Edison and followed across much of the Northeast.
