Rail Connections

North Edison sits directly on the Northeast rail spine.

Metropark and Edison stations connect the area to New York, Newark, Central New Jersey and Trenton, while Amtrak at Metropark opens the same corridor toward Philadelphia, Baltimore, Washington, New England and selected Pennsylvania destinations.

A Rail Corridor with Regional Reach

Commuter rail and intercity rail meet close to home.

The rail advantage of North Edison is not simply that trains run to Manhattan. The area sits beside the Northeast Corridor, the passenger rail axis linking the major cities of the Northeast and Mid Atlantic. Metropark is particularly important because NJ Transit and Amtrak use the same station, allowing everyday regional travel and longer intercity trips to begin at one location.

Metropark

The key rail gateway for North Edison.

Metropark is both an NJ Transit Northeast Corridor station and an Amtrak intercity station. NJ Transit places it at 100 Middlesex Turnpike in Iselin, directly off Garden State Parkway Exit 131A.

NJ Transit

Everyday movement along the Northeast Corridor.

NJ Transit's Northeast Corridor Line connects Metropark northward with Rahway, Elizabeth, Newark and New York, and southward with New Brunswick, Princeton Junction, Hamilton and Trenton. Current schedules also show Northeast Corridor service through Newark Liberty International Airport station on many trips.

This makes Metropark useful for far more than a traditional rush hour commute. It can serve work trips, airport access, day trips, events in New York, travel to New Brunswick and Princeton area destinations, and connections farther south at Trenton.

Amtrak

Intercity rail without first going to Manhattan.

Amtrak's Metropark station page identifies Acela, Northeast Regional, Keystone Service and long distance Northeast services among the routes associated with the station. Actual stopping patterns vary by train and day, so the practical value is not that every train stops at Metropark, but that selected intercity trains do.

A traveler can therefore begin many Northeast trips in the North Edison area rather than driving to New York, Newark or Philadelphia solely to board an intercity train.

NJ Transit Northeast Corridor

New York in one direction. Central Jersey and Trenton in the other.

Both Metropark and Edison are on NJ Transit's Northeast Corridor Line, giving the North Edison area more than one station option for regional rail travel.

North toward Newark and New York

Metropark trains run toward Newark Penn Station and New York Penn Station, placing Midtown Manhattan on a direct commuter rail axis. Newark also provides connections to PATH, local transit and additional rail services.

Newark Liberty Airport

Many Northeast Corridor trains serve Newark Liberty International Airport station. Travelers should verify current AirTrain arrangements, construction impacts and airport access procedures before relying on a particular connection.

South through Central New Jersey

Southbound service links the area with New Brunswick, Princeton Junction, Hamilton and Trenton. At Trenton, passengers can continue into southeastern Pennsylvania using SEPTA or transfer to additional Amtrak services.

Amtrak from Metropark

The larger Northeast opens from a suburban station.

Selected Amtrak trains stop at Metropark. Because schedules and stopping patterns change, the safest way to describe the station is as an entry point to the Amtrak Northeast network rather than as a guarantee of direct service on every route.

Northbound

New York, Connecticut, Rhode Island and Boston.

Amtrak's Northeast Corridor services connect New York with Stamford, New Haven, Providence, Route 128 and Boston. Current Acela timetables show selected trains stopping at Metropark and continuing north through the corridor. Northeast Regional service also links the major cities of the corridor, with individual stop patterns varying by train.

For North Edison, the significance is straightforward: Boston and southern New England are part of the same passenger rail system that passes through Metropark.

Southbound

Philadelphia, Wilmington, Baltimore and Washington.

The Northeast Corridor continues south through Philadelphia's William H. Gray III 30th Street Station, Wilmington, Baltimore and Washington Union Station. Selected Acela and Northeast Regional services serving Metropark provide direct or one train access to major destinations on this axis, depending on the train chosen.

That gives Philadelphia and Washington a rail option that avoids driving the full I-95 corridor and places travelers at central city rail stations with onward local transit connections.

Across Pennsylvania

Harrisburg can be reached on selected Keystone service. Pittsburgh requires a connection.

This distinction matters. It keeps the page useful and avoids overstating what Metropark itself serves.

Keystone Service

Philadelphia, Lancaster corridor and Harrisburg.

Amtrak's Keystone Service operates between New York, Philadelphia and Harrisburg. Current 2026 timetables show Metropark on selected Keystone trains, but many Keystone trains bypass the station. Depending on the chosen service, travel west of Philadelphia can include Pennsylvania stops such as Paoli, Exton, Lancaster and Harrisburg.

Because Metropark stops are limited, the exact itinerary should always be checked before travel rather than assuming every Keystone train is available locally.

Pennsylvanian

Pittsburgh is in the network, but not as a direct Metropark stop.

Amtrak's daily Pennsylvanian links New York and Pittsburgh through Philadelphia, Harrisburg, Altoona and other Pennsylvania communities. The current timetable lists Trenton and Newark but not Metropark.

For a North Edison traveler, Pittsburgh therefore requires an appropriate connection, potentially at Newark, Trenton, Philadelphia or another station depending on the itinerary. It is a useful rail option, but it should not be presented as a direct Metropark train.

Edison Station

A second nearby Northeast Corridor station.

Metropark is the stronger intercity gateway because Amtrak stops there, but Edison Station adds flexibility for NJ Transit travel.

NJ Transit service on the same corridor.

NJ Transit identifies Edison Station as a Northeast Corridor station. Its trains operate on the same regional rail spine toward New Brunswick and Trenton to the south and toward Newark and New York to the north.

Depending on the starting point, parking situation, schedule and traffic on local roads, Edison can be a practical alternative to Metropark for NJ Transit trips.

Different stations for different trips.

For intercity Amtrak travel, Metropark is the clear local focus. For routine NJ Transit travel, both Edison and Metropark deserve consideration. This two station choice is another reason North Edison's rail access is more useful than a simple distance to New York might suggest.

Beyond a Single Train

Connections extend the practical rail map.

The value of the Northeast Corridor grows further when major transfer points are considered.

New York

Moynihan Train Hall and Penn Station connect with the New York City subway, Long Island Rail Road, NJ Transit and other local transportation. Amtrak's Empire Service also begins in New York for the Hudson Valley, Albany and destinations across upstate New York.

Trenton and Philadelphia

Trenton provides a connection between NJ Transit and SEPTA, while Philadelphia is a major Amtrak transfer point for Pennsylvania and long distance services.

Washington and beyond

Some Northeast Regional trains continue beyond Washington into Virginia. Whether a particular trip can be made on one train from Metropark depends on the selected service and current timetable.

Planning Note

Check the train, not just the route name.

NJ Transit and Amtrak schedules change, and different trains on the same named route may make different stops. Amtrak service at Metropark is especially train specific. Before departure, verify the exact train, station stop, departure time, service alerts, parking information and any connection needed for the final destination.

Ground Connections: Rail

A suburban location with city center rail reach.

Metropark brings commuter and intercity rail together near North Edison. Edison Station adds a second NJ Transit option. Together they place New York, Philadelphia and much of the Northeast Corridor within the same connected rail system, with onward routes reaching New England, Pennsylvania, Washington and beyond.
